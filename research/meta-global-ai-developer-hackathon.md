# Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon Research

Research date: 26 October 2026. Event year: 2026. Local planning timezone: Asia/Singapore.

The hackathon is a remote, worldwide event hosted by Meta and Cerebral Valley, with a stated $1,000,000 cash prize pool. Participation is free for selected applicants. A competitive submission needs a working product that uses Meta models, solves a meaningful problem within one of two themes, and clearly demonstrates the work created during the event. The submission deadline is **Wednesday 4 November, 9:00 PM ET**, equivalent to **Thursday 5 November, 10:00 AM Singapore time**. [Participant guide][guide] · [Event overview][event]

## Project requirements

### Themes and required Meta integration

Choose one of these problem statements:

- **Build for Your World:** Apply Meta models to a domain you know firsthand, solving a real problem or enabling something previously impractical.
- **Push the Frontier:** Apply Meta models to difficult problems in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials, science or physical systems.

The project must incorporate Meta models. Stage one judging specifically checks that the project reasonably fits a theme and uses Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weight models. Using a coding assistant alone should not be assumed to satisfy this requirement. [Participant guide, sections 1 and 8][guide]

### Tracks

| Track | Intended participants | Public description of emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indie Developer | Individual builders and students | Creativity, technical ambition and a working result |
| Startup | Teams and founders building a real product | Technical execution and production readiness |

Every project enters one track. Solo participation is allowed, and teams may have at most four participants. The weighted judging rubric below applies to the competition; the public track descriptions are qualitative guidance. [Event overview][event] · [Participant guide, sections 4 and 8][guide]

### New work and contribution boundaries

- Presented work must have been built during the hackathon.
- The demo must highlight only the features, code and functionality created during the event.
- Judges must be able to identify original contributions; failure can cause immediate disqualification at their discretion.
- Startups may build on existing products or codebases, provided the newly developed work is clearly identified.
- The submitted repository must be public. The guide labels this requirement “Open source,” but does not specify a repository license in that section.
- Code, data and assets must be used with the necessary rights, and projects must comply with legal, ethical and platform policies.

These requirements support maintaining a dated contribution log, recording any starting codebase, and mapping each demonstrated feature to work completed during the event. Those are suggested evidence practices, rather than additional organizer requirements. [Participant guide, section 4][guide]

### Project categories to avoid

The guide's sample anti-project list is explicitly non-exhaustive:

- AI-generated medical advice, including mental health advisors and skin blemish detectors.
- Basic retrieval-augmented generation applications.
- Simple image analyzers.
- Simple education chatbots.
- AI job application screeners.
- AI nutrition coaches.
- Personality analyzers.
- Sports analyzers or coaches.
- Anything political or involving war.

The guide describes these examples as generic projects or policy violations and asks participants to avoid building them. Novelty alone does not override the legal, rights or policy restrictions. [Participant guide, section 4][guide]

## Judging and project selection

Judging has three stages. Stage one is a pass/fail viability, theme-fit and Meta-model-use check. Stage two scores eligible submissions from 0 to 10 in each category. The top five projects in each track reach the live final round, where judges watch the demo videos and rank projects with the same rubric. [Participant guide, section 8][guide]

| Category | Weight | What judges assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impact | 30% | Real-world potential, beneficiaries, significance, likelihood of actual use and theme fit |
| Use of Meta AI Tooling | 25% | Creative use beyond basic integration and compelling model capabilities |
| Technical Depth and Execution | 20% | Engineering quality, thoughtful design and refinement |
| Demo | 25% | A working, impressive live product and an engaging video |

For planning, a weighted score can be calculated as `0.30 × impact + 0.25 × tooling + 0.20 × execution + 0.25 × demo`, with each input scored from 0 to 10. This is a calculation from the published weights, not a separately published scoring formula.

The practical implication is to choose a narrow problem with a visible outcome, make Meta models central to solving it, and reserve substantial time for a reliable demonstration. Tooling and demo together account for 50% of the published score; impact has the largest individual weight.

## Eligibility and application context

The event accepts at most 20,000 applicants, with rolling review and possible early closure. The overview gives equal weight to project clarity, feasibility and theme fit, and to demonstrated technical skill. Experience links help verification but are optional. Questions 13–16 are excluded from selection scoring. Duplicate applications, false identities and spam can be disqualified. [Event overview][event]

Participation is prohibited where local law bars participation or receiving an award. The overview excludes sanctioned persons or organizations, those acting for them, and those organized or located in Belarus, China, Russia, Ukraine, Syria, Cuba, North Korea, Iran, or the listed sanctioned Ukrainian regions of Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk. It references US, EU, UN and UK sanctions and export restrictions. Preserve the organizer's exact eligibility conditions when assessing an individual entrant. [Event overview][event]

Meta and Cerebral Valley employees, contractors and interns, government-affiliated individuals, their immediate families and people in the same household are ineligible. The overview defines immediate family broadly, including spouses, domestic partners, parents, guardians, wards, children, siblings and their respective spouses. [Event overview][event]

The supplied rules additionally state that entrants must be **18 or older and of legal age where they live**. This age condition comes from the user's waiver summary; the linked [application overview and waiver](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon/waiver) was not independently read for this note. The event overview also says selected participants must accept the official rules.

## Submission checklist

Submit through the [CV submission page][submit] by **4 November, 9:00 PM ET / 5 November, 10:00 AM Singapore time**. Late submissions are not considered. Teams and startups appoint one representative and submit one project rather than one submission per person. Judges use the three-minute video, code and live product. [Participant guide, section 7][guide]

- [ ] Choose one track and identify the theme addressed.
- [ ] Keep team membership within the four-person limit.
- [ ] Make the repository publicly accessible.
- [ ] Clearly identify work created during the hackathon and distinguish any pre-existing product or code.
- [ ] Include a three-minute demo video showing the newly built features, code and functionality.
- [ ] Verify that the video link works for judges.
- [ ] Verify that the live product and demonstrated workflow work.
- [ ] Add everyone who worked on the project to the submission.
- [ ] Have the designated representative submit before the deadline.

## Recommended build preparation

These are planning recommendations derived from the rules and rubric:

1. Resolve the eligible build-start date with organizers and record the project's initial state.
2. Select a firsthand problem with a measurable outcome and a compelling three-minute demonstration.
3. Confirm actual model access and run a minimal request before committing the architecture to a model.
4. Claim individual credits early enough for batch distribution, then monitor usage.
5. Keep a contribution log and a simple list of third-party code, datasets and assets with their rights or licenses.
6. Build the complete demonstration workflow early, then improve reliability and technical depth.
7. Prepare the public repository, contribution explanation and video before the final submission window; use 3 November ET for troubleshooting.
